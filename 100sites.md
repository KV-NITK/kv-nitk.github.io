RASTER is a poster machine that sets one Weltformat-style sheet on off-white paper (#f2efe8) using only signal red #e30613, black #111 and white. It picks a zone split, where the display type sits beside or above a column of small type, and one of seven motifs: Zahl (a numeral as tall as the zone with an overprinted red sun), Drei Sprachen (one word stacked in German, French and Italian), Bogen (concentric quarter arcs in the manner of the Beethoven posters), Kreis (disc, moon and a word across the base), Takt (a time signature above bars), Senkrecht (a word climbing a strip beside a halftone field of dots) and Diagonale (a band of rotated bars). Small type is set flush left with its cap height hung from the grid line. It includes a bilingual headline, a red date (sometimes knocked out of a red panel), ragged body columns, an edition mark ("No. 0417") and a rotated imprint caption that says the programme is fictional.

Harmonia — Pendulum Wave

Harmonia presents the classic pendulum-wave apparatus as a quiet piece of kinetic art on a charcoal (#16171b) stage. Eighteen bifilar pendulums (each bob hangs from two fine white strings in a V, so it swings in a single plane) hang from a brushed-metal bar seen in a slightly elevated three-quarter perspective. Their lengths come from L = g(T/2π)² so that they complete exactly 51, 52 … 68 oscillations in 60 seconds (34.4 cm down to 19.3 cm). Every bob starts from the same 5.5 cm throw and follows the exact small-angle solution, so the snake, the two-, three- and four-row splits, the chaos and the realignment at 60 s all come out of the physics rather than from choreography. The glowing spheres run through a spectrum from peach #ffd6a5 to ice #9bf6ff, and optional light trails sweep out translucent silk ribbons as the wave line moves through space. Beneath the stage, a monospace instrument console shows elapsed time, cycle phase, the named pattern, the neighbour phase offset, an orrery-like phase dial, and a scrubbable 60-second timeline marked with the pattern regions and the ¼ ⅓ ½ ⅔ ¾ split points.
